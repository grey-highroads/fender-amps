## Visual construction

Angled two-piece V-front baffle with a chrome strip down the centre holding the two halves. Finger-jointed pine cabinet, early tweed covering described as white or linen, dark brown grille. Top-facing chrome control panel. Nailed-on logo plate on the JB recreation. Open back with the tubes hanging from the chassis.

**Specific to the selected year:** The V-front and centre strip are specific to 1947 to about 1952 (Dual Professional and V-front Super). The tube lineup with 6SJ7s is specific to the Dual Professional name.

## Modeling priorities

Five features that most distinguish this amp:

1. V-front split baffle and its angle
2. Vertical chrome centre strip
3. Top-facing chrome panel with three knobs
4. Pale early tweed against a dark grille
5. Two 10-inch speakers angled apart

**Likely reusable parts:** tweed covering (needs an early white/linen variant); speaker-jensen-p12r construction as a starting point for 10-inch field-era Jensens; pointer knobs from the 5E3 assembly.

**Still estimates:** Overall envelope rests on one dealer figure and one recreation spec. Baffle angle and side taper have no figures. Speaker models. Handle and feet.

## What's cool about it

- It is the first Fender with two speakers, and by most accounts the first guitar amp anywhere built that way. [gw-dualpro](https://www.guitarworld.com/reviews/fender-48-dual-professional-jb-edition-review) [bb-dualpro](https://bluebookofguitarvalues.com/products/fender-dual-professional-super)
- A lot of what you think of as "a Fender amp" starts here: tweed covering, the top-facing chrome panel, and the finger-jointed pine box. [gw-dualpro](https://www.guitarworld.com/reviews/fender-48-dual-professional-jb-edition-review) [rv-dualpro-1947](https://reverb.com/item/3184036)
- The front is not flat. Each speaker sits on its own baffle and the two are angled apart, so the amp throws sound wider than a flat 2x10. [gw-dualpro](https://www.guitarworld.com/reviews/fender-48-dual-professional-jb-edition-review)
- One restored example runs a separate output transformer for each speaker. If that is how they left the factory, it is a very unusual way to drive a combo. [rv-dualpro-1947](https://reverb.com/item/3184036)

## Uncertainty

- Second pass: the amp is no longer blocked. One 1947 dealer figure and the 2023 recreation spec now give a working envelope. Neither is a factory dimension for the vintage amp.
- The 1947 specimen figure was read from a search excerpt because the archive page would not open. Re-check it at the source when possible.
- Baffle angle and side taper are visible in photos but unmeasured.
- Introduction is given as 1946, late 1946, 1947, or "only offered in 1947" depending on the source. The catalog says 1947 to 1948.
- Output is 16 W in Blue Book, 18 W in the catalog and 26 W in Mojotone. The 2023 recreation is rated 26 W. Do not use 26 W as the vintage figure without a period source.
- Preamp is three 6SJ7 in Blue Book and two in Mojotone.
- Dual output transformers now have two secondary sources and one restored specimen photo. Still not a factory document. Do not present as a universal factory fact.
- Avoid "first twin-speaker amplifier ever" without attribution. Sources say it; none is a primary document.
