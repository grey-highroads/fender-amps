## Visual construction

Small narrow-panel tweed box, top chrome panel with a single chicken-head knob, two input jacks, red pilot jewel, leather handle. Open back with a small chassis and three tubes.

**Specific to the selected year:** The 8-inch speaker and the slightly larger cabinet separate it from the 6-inch 5E1 and early 5F1.

## Modeling priorities

Five features that most distinguish this amp:

1. Scale: it should look tiny next to a Deluxe
2. Single knob on the top panel
3. 8-inch speaker behind the grille
4. Three-tube open back
5. Tweed and oxblood grille matching the 5E3

**Likely reusable parts:** deluxe-5e3 assembly scaled down (tweed, oxblood grille, chicken-head knob, top chrome panel); new 8-inch speaker needed.

**Still estimates:** Baffle cutout, chassis size, handle and feet.

## What's cool about it

- Three tubes and one knob. There is nothing between your guitar and the speaker to hide behind. [fsup](https://support.fender.com/hc/en-gb/articles/42507659450779-What-tubes-are-in-my-amp) [mojo-champ](https://mojotone.com/blogs/fender-field-guide/fender-narrow-panel-tweed-champ)
- It is single-ended: one power tube doing all the work, so it distorts in a different, more lopsided way than any push-pull Fender. [fsup](https://support.fender.com/hc/en-gb/articles/42507659450779-What-tubes-are-in-my-amp)
- The Champ grew up in stages. The 5E1 gained a choke and negative feedback, and the 5F1 then dropped the choke again. [mojo-champ](https://mojotone.com/blogs/fender-field-guide/fender-narrow-panel-tweed-champ)
- The cabinet got bigger when the speaker went from six inches to eight, which is a good part of why later Champs sound fuller. [mojo-champ](https://mojotone.com/blogs/fender-field-guide/fender-narrow-panel-tweed-champ)

## Uncertainty

- The catalog starts this row in 1958. Ampwares, Fender and guitar-list date the 5F1 from 1956. This is a split by speaker size, not an error, but the timeline year needs a decision.
- Load: 4 ohms nominal or 3.2 ohms as marked on vintage speakers.
- Second pass: a measured April 1959 specimen is 12 x 13.5 x 7.5 in. The field guide says 12.5 x 13.5 x 8 and a reproduction cabinet is larger again.
- The Fender 5F1 schematic was not read in this pass.
- Famous-recording claims about the tweed Champ are common and poorly sourced. Avoid naming records.
