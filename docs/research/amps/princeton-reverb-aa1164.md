## Visual construction

A small black-panel combo with one ten. Six skirted knobs, two inputs, black strap handle, two chrome strips on the top. The earliest amps have no grille logo; the raised logo was being fitted by early 1965.

**Specific to the selected year:** 1965 straddles the logo change. The main modeling specimen (RetroFret A-03894, output transformer week 7 of 1965) has the logo, and a very early 1965 amp does not. See the [modeling packet](../packets/princeton-reverb-modeling.md).

## Modeling priorities

Five features that most distinguish this amp:

1. One ten in a small cabinet
2. Six knobs and a single channel
3. No middle control and no bright switch
4. No grille logo on a 1965
5. Small transformers on the chassis

**Likely reusable parts:** deluxe-reverb-black parts: black tolex, silver grille, skirted knobs, black panel, strap handle, glides; new 10-inch speaker; new smaller cabinet and six-knob single-channel panel.

**Still estimates:** All interior positions. A dated early 1965 specimen is now listed for the exterior, but it has no chassis photos.

## What's cool about it

- It looks like a small Deluxe Reverb, but one tube stage is different and that changes the whole amp. [rr-aa1164](https://robrobinette.com/AA1164_Princeton_Reverb.htm)
- The tremolo works on the power tubes, not the signal, so it can go deeper than the tremolo on bigger blackface amps. [pg-pr](https://www.premierguitar.com/diy/silver-and-black/fender-princeton-reverb-2651250510) [rr-aa1164](https://robrobinette.com/AA1164_Princeton_Reverb.htm)
- There is no choke in the power supply, which makes it softer and spongier under the fingers. [rr-aa1164](https://robrobinette.com/AA1164_Princeton_Reverb.htm)
- Because the tremolo does not load the signal, the stage after the reverb gets a hotter feed than it does in a Deluxe Reverb. [rr-aa1164](https://robrobinette.com/AA1164_Princeton_Reverb.htm)

## Hotspot proposals

### 1. Half a tube runs the power section

- **Anchor:** Chassis, fourth preamp tube
- **Opening insight:** The phase inverter is half of a 12AX7. Bigger blackface amps give that job a whole 12AT7.
- **Why it matters to a player:** It runs out of clean drive early. That is where the Princeton's loose low end and brown breakup come from.
- **Deeper notes:** A cathodyne inverter with total gain under 2. Driven hard it produces double-frequency blocking distortion. The other half of the tube runs the tremolo.
- **Schematic check:** Confirmed. The inverter is drawn as half a 12AX7 with a 56k plate load, a 56k cathode load and a 1k bias resistor. The other half of that tube is the tremolo oscillator. Gain and distortion behaviour are analysis. [fender-aa1164-schem](https://www.thetubestore.com/lib/thetubestore/schematics/Fender/Fender-Princeton-Reverb-AA1164-Schematic.pdf)
- **Sources:** [rr-aa1164](https://robrobinette.com/AA1164_Princeton_Reverb.htm) [pg-pr](https://www.premierguitar.com/diy/silver-and-black/fender-princeton-reverb-2651250510) [fg-pr](https://fenderguru.com/amp-models/princeton-reverb/)

### 2. Tremolo that moves the power tubes

- **Anchor:** Front panel, Speed and Intensity knobs
- **Opening insight:** This tremolo does not turn the signal up and down. It swings the bias of the power tubes.
- **Why it matters to a player:** It can go deeper and feels more like the amp is breathing. It also weakens if the tubes are biased hot.
- **Deeper notes:** An oscillating voltage is added to the fixed bias on the 6V6 grids. The factory bias is relatively cool, which suits the effect.
- **Schematic check:** Confirmed. The Intensity pot (250K-L) feeds the oscillator output into the bias line for the 6V6 grids. The bias supply has a diode and fixed resistors with no adjustment pot. [fender-aa1164-schem](https://www.thetubestore.com/lib/thetubestore/schematics/Fender/Fender-Princeton-Reverb-AA1164-Schematic.pdf)
- **Sources:** [rr-aa1164](https://robrobinette.com/AA1164_Princeton_Reverb.htm) [pg-pr](https://www.premierguitar.com/diy/silver-and-black/fender-princeton-reverb-2651250510) [gcom-biastrem](https://guitar.com/workshop/diy-workshop-rift-amp-mod-part-2/)

### 3. No choke

- **Anchor:** Chassis underside, power supply
- **Opening insight:** Where other blackface amps have a choke in the power supply, the Princeton Reverb has none.
- **Why it matters to a player:** The supply is softer, so the amp sags and compresses more when you play hard.
- **Deeper notes:** Filtering uses a multi-section can capacitor (20/20/20/20 µF). The source gives about 320 V and 240 V at two supply nodes; these are analysis figures.
- **Schematic check:** Confirmed. No choke is drawn. Four 20 µF 450 V sections are joined by a 1k 1 W and two 18k 1 W resistors. The drawing marks about 420 V at the rectifier, 400 V on the screens, 320 V and 240 V at the later nodes, all within 20%. So the 320 V and 240 V figures are Fender's. [fender-aa1164-schem](https://www.thetubestore.com/lib/thetubestore/schematics/Fender/Fender-Princeton-Reverb-AA1164-Schematic.pdf)
- **Sources:** [rr-aa1164](https://robrobinette.com/AA1164_Princeton_Reverb.htm)

### 4. Two tone knobs, no Middle

- **Anchor:** Front panel, Treble and Bass knobs
- **Opening insight:** There is no middle control and no bright switch.
- **Why it matters to a player:** The mids are set by a fixed resistor, so there is no panel control for them. Players who want more or less mids change the guitar, the speaker or the pedal in front.
- **Deeper notes:** The fixed mid value was not confirmed in this pass. Do not print one until the schematic is checked.
- **Schematic check:** Tone stack confirmed as Treble and Bass only: 250 pF treble capacitor, .1 and .047 µF capacitors, 100k slope resistor. The fixed resistor from the Bass pot to ground reads as 6800 ohms, but the scan is coarse at that point. Confirm on a cleaner copy before printing it. [fender-aa1164-schem](https://www.thetubestore.com/lib/thetubestore/schematics/Fender/Fender-Princeton-Reverb-AA1164-Schematic.pdf)
- **Sources:** [mojo-pr](https://mojotone.com/blogs/fender-field-guide/fender-blackface-princeton-reverb)

### 5. Reverb with more drive after it

- **Anchor:** Cabinet floor, reverb tank
- **Opening insight:** The reverb circuit is close to the bigger amps, but what comes after it is not.
- **Why it matters to a player:** With no signal tremolo loading the line, the next gain stage is hit harder, which adds to the amp's push.
- **Deeper notes:** A 12AT7 drives the tank. A 3.3M resistor sets the dry mix, the same job it does in the larger blackface amps.
- **Schematic check:** Confirmed. Both halves of a 12AT7 in parallel drive the reverb transformer through a 500 pF capacitor. The dry path passes a 3.3M resistor with a 10 pF capacitor across it. [fender-aa1164-schem](https://www.thetubestore.com/lib/thetubestore/schematics/Fender/Fender-Princeton-Reverb-AA1164-Schematic.pdf)
- **Sources:** [rr-aa1164](https://robrobinette.com/AA1164_Princeton_Reverb.htm) [fg-pr](https://fenderguru.com/amp-models/princeton-reverb/)

### 6. Bare power tubes

- **Anchor:** Chassis, the two 6V6 power tubes
- **Opening insight:** The 6V6s have no grid-stopper or screen resistors, and the bias is fixed with no adjustment.
- **Why it matters to a player:** It is a simpler, less protected output stage than the Deluxe Reverb's, and it responds to tube choice.
- **Deeper notes:** Negative feedback is taken through a 2.7k resistor. An adjustable bias pot is a common modification, not a factory part.
- **Schematic check:** Confirmed. No grid-stopper or screen resistors are drawn on the 6V6GTs; the screens tie straight to the 400 V node. Grid leaks are 220k 5%. Feedback is 2700 ohms into 47 ohms. [fender-aa1164-schem](https://www.thetubestore.com/lib/thetubestore/schematics/Fender/Fender-Princeton-Reverb-AA1164-Schematic.pdf)
- **Sources:** [rr-aa1164](https://robrobinette.com/AA1164_Princeton_Reverb.htm)

### 7. A very small output transformer

- **Anchor:** Chassis, output transformer
- **Opening insight:** The output transformer is small and sized for an 8-ohm load.
- **Why it matters to a player:** It limits clean headroom and low end, which is why a Deluxe Reverb transformer is a well-known upgrade.
- **Deeper notes:** No factory part number was found in this pass.
- **Schematic check:** Corrected. The first pass found no part number. The schematic lists the output transformer as TR2, 125A10B. Its impedances are not on the drawing. [fender-aa1164-schem](https://www.thetubestore.com/lib/thetubestore/schematics/Fender/Fender-Princeton-Reverb-AA1164-Schematic.pdf)
- **Sources:** [fg-pr](https://fenderguru.com/amp-models/princeton-reverb/) [rr-aa1164](https://robrobinette.com/AA1164_Princeton_Reverb.htm)

### 8. One ten

- **Anchor:** Cabinet front, speaker
- **Opening insight:** Stock speakers were Jensen C10R or C10N, or Oxford 10L5 or 10J4.
- **Why it matters to a player:** The ten is part of the Princeton's identity, and a 12-inch baffle is the classic owner upgrade. One 1966 seller who fitted an Altec 417-8C 12-inch says it gave tighter bass, more headroom and a more open sound than the stock ten. That is one owner comparing two specific speakers, not a rule about sizes. [rv-pr-altec](https://reverb.com/item/98457702-fender-princeton-reverb-12-watt-1x10-guitar-combo-1964-1967-black-panel)
- **Deeper notes:** Fitting a 12-inch baffle is a common change. Model the factory ten.
- **Schematic check:** Not a schematic fact. The drawing shows one speaker and an extension jack with no model named. [fender-aa1164-schem](https://www.thetubestore.com/lib/thetubestore/schematics/Fender/Fender-Princeton-Reverb-AA1164-Schematic.pdf)
- **Sources:** [mojo-pr](https://mojotone.com/blogs/fender-field-guide/fender-blackface-princeton-reverb) [fg-pr](https://fenderguru.com/amp-models/princeton-reverb/)
