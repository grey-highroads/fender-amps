## Visual construction

Two pieces: a low, wide head sitting on a large closed cabinet. Blonde tolex, oxblood grille, brown forward-facing control panel. The speaker is mounted on a "tone ring", a second baffle whose shadow is visible through the cloth on a clean example.

**Specific to the selected year:** Rough blonde tolex is described as pre-1963 and smooth blonde from 1963. Oxblood grille gives way to wheat late in the run. Knob colour changed during production. Both 1961 specimens viewed in this pass have light-coloured knobs on the brown panel; confirm the exact colour at full size.

## Modeling priorities

Five features that most distinguish this amp:

1. Piggyback stance: head narrower than the cabinet
2. Blonde tolex with oxblood grille
3. Brown front panel with two channels plus tremolo
4. Tone ring behind the grille
5. Tilt-back legs or head-securing hardware, once confirmed

**Likely reusable parts:** brown-era barrel knobs and forward-facing brown panel from the brown Princeton and Vibroverb studies; oxblood grille; new blonde tolex material, piggyback head and separate cabinet.

**Still estimates:** Knob colour for 1961, head-to-cabinet fastening, tone-ring geometry, cabinet depth including hardware.

## What's cool about it

- This is where Fender splits the amp in two. Getting the chassis out of the speaker box is what made a very loud, clean amp practical in 1960. [mojo-showman](https://mojotone.com/blogs/fender-field-guide/fender-blonde-showman?page=2) [vg-showman](https://www.vintageguitar.com/34092/fenders-1961-showman/amp/)
- The speaker does not bolt to the front baffle. It hangs on a tone ring inside a sealed cabinet, which tightens the low end compared with an open-back combo. [rv-showman-1962](https://reverb.com/item/84210985-1961-fender-showman-6g14-a) [mahar-tonering](https://www.maharsvintageguitars.com/blogs/news/fender-tone-ring-white-tolex-amps)
- It shipped with JBLs, not Jensens or Oxfords. That is a big part of the hard, clear attack people associate with surf guitar. [rv-showman-1962](https://reverb.com/item/84210985-1961-fender-showman-6g14-a) [mojo-showman](https://mojotone.com/blogs/fender-field-guide/fender-blonde-showman?page=2)
- Electrically it is close to the blonde Twin with a different output transformer, in a format that can drive a single fifteen. [vg-showman](https://www.vintageguitar.com/34092/fenders-1961-showman/amp/)

## Uncertainty

- Which cabinet to model is now a choice, not a gap. Two 1961 6G14-A amps eight serial numbers apart are documented, one as a 12 and one as a 15. The catalog says "typically 1x15". The 12 has far more photos.
- Speaker model for the 15: one specimen points to a D130. Mojotone says D140F. No Fender document was read.
- Corrected from first pass: the Reverb listing was cited for D130F fifteens. It is a Showman 12 with a replacement K120. The D130 evidence comes from the gbase listing instead.
- Corrected from first pass: the Reverb source was labelled 1962. The seller dates it 1961.
- Published cabinet sizes still come from one field guide. Neither specimen listing gives dimensions.
- Output rating: 80, 85 or 100 W.
- Dick Dale and the Showman are closely linked in the literature, but no source for that was read. Do not state it yet.
- Whether the earliest 6G14 used two or four output tubes. The dealer page that said two is gone. Not verified.
- The Fender 6G14-A schematic was not read in this pass.
