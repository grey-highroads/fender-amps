## Visual construction

Solid hardwood cabinet with rounded edges, built from 1-inch boards Fender bought for lap steels: blonde maple, black walnut, dark mahogany, occasionally oak. Grille cloth red, blue or yellow-gold, with a matching cloth lower back panel. Three vertical metal strips over the grille (a wide center strip; some amps have two). Wooden handle on top. The chassis hangs from the top with the tubes pointing down, and the control panel faces the back.

The rear panel is stenciled: INST. VOL., the FENDER ELECTRIC lightning-bolt logo, "INSTRUMENT CO. Fullerton California SERIAL NO. ___ MODEL 26", MIC. VOL., a Microphone jack, two Instruments jacks, and TONE with OFF/ON. Knobs are black pointers with a 1 to 12 scale. Early amps had no circuit board; Fender added boards in 1947.

**Specific to the selected year:** a 1946 amp should have the field-coil speaker and no circuit board. Both photo sets below show the later transformer-on-basket layout, so the 1946 interior is less well covered than the exterior.

## Modeling priorities

1. Hardwood cabinet with rounded edges and visible grain
2. Coloured grille cloth with three vertical metal strips
3. Rear-facing stenciled control panel with three black pointer knobs
4. Chassis hung from the top, tubes pointing down
5. Wooden handle

**Likely reusable parts:** none from the tweed studies; this is a new cabinet family. The Dual Professional brief (1947) is the nearest construction.

**Still estimates:** all dimensions, strip widths, handle shape.

## What's cool about it

- It's the first amp called Deluxe, and the circuit already has the tweed Deluxe's basic recipe: two cathode-biased 6V6s with no feedback. [m26_schem](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0604/9615/0624/files/Model_26_Deluxe.gif?v=1759171998)
- It takes a microphone. That fits an amp sold next to lap steels to players who also sang. This is our reading, not Fender's.
- The Tone knob works on the output tubes and doubles as the power switch. [m26_schem](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0604/9615/0624/files/Model_26_Deluxe.gif?v=1759171998) [fender_woodies](https://www.fender.com/articles/behind-the-scenes/the-woodies-a-history-of-fenders-first-amps)
- No two cabinets look quite the same, because Fender used whatever hardwood it had. [fender_woodies](https://www.fender.com/articles/behind-the-scenes/the-woodies-a-history-of-fenders-first-amps)
