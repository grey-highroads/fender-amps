## Visual construction

Rough brown tolex, wheat grille, raised brown leather handle, chrome glides, brown barrel knobs on a brown forward-facing panel. Rear panel carries the ground switch, fuse, power switch, speaker jacks and tremolo jack.

**Specific to the selected year:** A 1962 specimen viewed at full size reads, left to right: Normal inputs 1 and 2, Volume, Tone; Bright inputs 1 and 2, Volume, Tone; Speed, Intensity; then the "Deluxe-Amp" script and pilot lamp. No baffle screws are visible on its front. Its rear panel reads Ground, Fuse 2 Amp, a single On switch, Speaker, Ext. Spkr. The first pass noted that early examples had a front-mounted baffle and the channels in the other order. No early example was found to confirm that.

## Modeling priorities

Five features that most distinguish this amp:

1. Brown panel with six knobs and no reverb
2. Narrower cabinet than a Deluxe Reverb
3. Wheat grille against brown tolex
4. Brown barrel knobs
5. Leather handle, not the later strap

**Likely reusable parts:** brown tolex, wheat grille and brown barrel knobs from the brown Princeton and Vibroverb studies; speaker-oxford-12k5; front-facing panel layout approach from deluxe-reverb-black.

**Still estimates:** Panel artwork and typography, knob shade, baffle fixing for the selected year.

## What's cool about it

- It sits right between tweed and blackface: fixed bias and negative feedback like the later amps, but still raw enough that people call it Fender's "mini Marshall". [tme-6g3-1962](https://themusicemporium.com/products/fender-6g3-deluxe-1962) [gcom-6g3](https://guitar.com/reviews/vintage-review/vintage-bench-test-1960s-fender-deluxe-shootout/)
- It is the first Deluxe with tremolo, and the tremolo works by pulsing the power tubes' bias, not by chopping the signal. [gp-6g3](https://guitarplayer.com/gear/the-1962-fender-6g3-deluxe-could-be-all-the-amp-you-ever-need) [gcom-6g3](https://guitar.com/reviews/vintage-review/vintage-bench-test-1960s-fender-deluxe-shootout/)
- Each channel gets its own volume and tone, so unlike the tweed Deluxe the controls stay out of each other's way. [mojo-6g3](https://mojotone.com/blogs/fender-field-guide/fender-brownface-deluxe)
- The bias is fixed and not adjustable, and the tremolo depends on it. That link is why bias changes affect the tremolo. [mojo-6g3](https://mojotone.com/blogs/fender-field-guide/fender-brownface-deluxe) [gp-6g3](https://guitarplayer.com/gear/the-1962-fender-6g3-deluxe-could-be-all-the-amp-you-ever-need)

## Uncertainty

- Second pass: rectifier is GZ34 on the Fender schematic. Settled.
- Output: 15 or 20 W.
- Channel order on a 1962 amp is Normal then Bright, left to right. Whether earlier amps differ is unconfirmed; no 1961 specimen was viewed.
- Baffle fixing: no front screws visible on the 1962 specimen. Not checked from inside the cabinet.
- One seller lists a standby switch. The Fender schematic draws one AC switch and the other specimen's rear panel shows one switch. Model one.
- The schematic was read at low zoom. Small component values were not checked.
- Changed from first pass: "The schematic link was not opened" no longer applies. The 5Y3 specimen claim could not be found in the dealer text read this time.
