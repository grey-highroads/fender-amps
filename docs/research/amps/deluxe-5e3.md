## Visual construction

Narrow-panel tweed, top chrome panel numbered 1 to 12, three chicken-head knobs, four inputs. Chassis hangs from the top with the tubes pointing down. Speaker jacks sit under the chassis.

**Specific to the selected year:** Later amps carry a logo tag with extra wording. Grille is brown or oxblood with gold stripe depending on year.

## Modeling priorities

Five features that most distinguish this amp:

1. Three knobs that interact
2. Top-hung chassis with tubes down
3. Small output transformer
4. 5Y3 rectifier
5. Jensen P12R

**Likely reusable parts:** deluxe-5e3 (existing assembly); speaker-jensen-p12r.

**Still estimates:** Interior component positions. The analysis page gives a signal path, not measured locations in a specific amp.

## What's cool about it

- The volume knob for the channel you are not using still changes your sound. [rr-5e3](https://robrobinette.com/How_The_5E3_Deluxe_Works.htm)
- There is no negative feedback at all, which is a big part of why it goes from clean to dirty so gradually. [rr-5e3](https://robrobinette.com/How_The_5E3_Deluxe_Works.htm)
- It uses the simplest kind of phase inverter Fender made, and it adds almost no gain of its own. [rr-5e3](https://robrobinette.com/How_The_5E3_Deluxe_Works.htm)
- The output transformer is small enough to saturate, so the amp compresses on its own when pushed. [rr-5e3](https://robrobinette.com/How_The_5E3_Deluxe_Works.htm)

## Hotspot proposals

Superseded on 2026-11-20 by the [5E3 explorer packet](../packets/deluxe-5e3-explorer.md), which rechecks every card against the Fender drawing and uses Fender's panel names (Instrument and Microphone). Kept below for the record.

### 1. Two volumes, one sound

- **Anchor:** Top panel, the two volume knobs
- **Opening insight:** Turn the volume of the channel you are not plugged into and your tone changes anyway.
- **Why it matters to a player:** It gives you a second gain and tone control for free. Many players set the unused volume by ear and leave it.
- **Deeper notes:** The unused channel's volume pot and its 100k plate load form a load on the channel in use that ranges from about 100k at maximum to 1M at minimum, which changes stage gain.
- **Schematic check:** Confirmed in part. The Fender schematic shows two 100k plate loads and two 1 MEG volume pots. The 100k to 1M load range is the analyst's reading of that network, not a figure on the drawing. [fender-5e3-schem](https://www.thetubestore.com/lib/thetubestore/schematics/Fender/Fender-Deluxe-5E3-Schematic.pdf)
- **Sources:** [rr-5e3](https://robrobinette.com/How_The_5E3_Deluxe_Works.htm) [rr-inputs](https://robrobinette.com/How_Fender_Input_Jacks_Work.htm)

### 2. A tone control that depends on the volumes

- **Anchor:** Top panel, tone knob
- **Opening insight:** With the Bright volume all the way down, the tone knob can no longer reach its brightest setting.
- **Why it matters to a player:** It explains why the same tone setting sounds different from one day to the next: the other knobs moved.
- **Deeper notes:** The 500 pF bright cap ends up connected to the bright end of the tone pot and bleeds highs to ground when the Bright volume is at zero.
- **Schematic check:** Confirmed. A .0005 µF (500 pF) capacitor sits between a volume pot and the tone pot on the schematic, and the layout marks it ".0005 MICA". The tone pot also has a .005 µF to ground. What happens at zero volume is analysis. [fender-5e3-schem](https://www.thetubestore.com/lib/thetubestore/schematics/Fender/Fender-Deluxe-5E3-Schematic.pdf)
- **Sources:** [rr-5e3](https://robrobinette.com/How_The_5E3_Deluxe_Works.htm) [rr-inputs](https://robrobinette.com/How_Fender_Input_Jacks_Work.htm)

### 3. Jumpering the channels

- **Anchor:** Top panel, the four input jacks
- **Opening insight:** A short patch cable from one channel to the other runs both preamp halves side by side.
- **Why it matters to a player:** You get a thicker sound and can blend the two volumes.
- **Deeper notes:** Connecting Bright Lo to Normal Hi parallels the two halves of the first tube. At maximum volume the Bright and Normal channels are otherwise identical.
- **Schematic check:** Not a schematic fact. The drawing confirms four inputs, each pair with 68k resistors and a 1 MEG to ground. The jumper behaviour is from the analysis. [fender-5e3-schem](https://www.thetubestore.com/lib/thetubestore/schematics/Fender/Fender-Deluxe-5E3-Schematic.pdf)
- **Sources:** [rr-5e3](https://robrobinette.com/How_The_5E3_Deluxe_Works.htm)

### 4. A 12AY7 up front

- **Anchor:** Chassis, first preamp tube
- **Opening insight:** The first tube is a 12AY7, a lower-gain type than the 12AX7 most later Fenders use.
- **Why it matters to a player:** The first stage stays cleaner, so most of the dirt comes from further down the chain and from the power tubes.
- **Deeper notes:** Both halves share one 820-ohm cathode resistor with a 25 µF bypass capacitor.
- **Schematic check:** Confirmed. 12AY7 with one shared 820-ohm cathode resistor and a "25-25" bypass capacitor (25 µF, 25 V). [fender-5e3-schem](https://www.thetubestore.com/lib/thetubestore/schematics/Fender/Fender-Deluxe-5E3-Schematic.pdf)
- **Sources:** [rr-5e3](https://robrobinette.com/How_The_5E3_Deluxe_Works.htm) [mojo-5e3](https://mojotone.com/blogs/fender-field-guide/fender-narrow-panel-tweed-deluxe)

### 5. The split-load phase inverter

- **Anchor:** Chassis, second preamp tube
- **Opening insight:** Half of one tube does the job of feeding both power tubes, and it adds almost no gain doing it.
- **Why it matters to a player:** When you hit it hard it distorts in its own way, which is part of the 5E3's rough edge.
- **Deeper notes:** The second stage has a 1.5k cathode resistor with a 25 µF bypass. The inverter is a cathodyne. The source describes its gain as always slightly under 2.
- **Schematic check:** Confirmed. Second stage: 1500-ohm cathode resistor with a 25-25 bypass and a 100k plate load. Inverter: 56k plate load, 56k cathode load, 1500-ohm bias resistor and 1 MEG grid resistor. The gain figure is analysis. [fender-5e3-schem](https://www.thetubestore.com/lib/thetubestore/schematics/Fender/Fender-Deluxe-5E3-Schematic.pdf)
- **Sources:** [rr-5e3](https://robrobinette.com/How_The_5E3_Deluxe_Works.htm)

### 6. Cathode bias and no feedback

- **Anchor:** Chassis, the two 6V6 power tubes
- **Opening insight:** The two 6V6s share one cathode resistor and there is no negative feedback loop around them.
- **Why it matters to a player:** The feel is soft and round, and breakup arrives early and gradually.
- **Deeper notes:** Shared 250-ohm cathode resistor. The source does not give the bypass capacitor value, so do not print one.
- **Schematic check:** Confirmed, and the missing value is now found. Shared cathode resistor is 250 ohms, 5 W. Its bypass capacitor is 25 µF, 25 V on both the schematic and the layout. Each 6V6 grid has a 1500-ohm series resistor and a 220k grid leak. No feedback loop is drawn. [fender-5e3-schem](https://www.thetubestore.com/lib/thetubestore/schematics/Fender/Fender-Deluxe-5E3-Schematic.pdf)
- **Sources:** [rr-5e3](https://robrobinette.com/How_The_5E3_Deluxe_Works.htm)

### 7. A 5Y3 that gives under load

- **Anchor:** Chassis, rectifier tube
- **Opening insight:** The rectifier is a 5Y3, and the supply voltage drops when you dig in.
- **Why it matters to a player:** That drop is the "sag" players feel as a bloom after the pick attack.
- **Deeper notes:** The analysis lists about 370 V at the rectifier, 295 V at the screens and 250 V for the preamp. These are from a circuit analysis, not measurements of the modelled amp.
- **Schematic check:** Rectifier confirmed as 5Y3GT. Voltages not found. The layout carries a notice that voltages are read to ground within 20%, but no voltage figures were legible on the copy read. Keep the 370, 295 and 250 V figures labelled as analysis. The supply is three 16 µF 450 V capacitors with 5000-ohm and 22k dropping resistors. [fender-5e3-schem](https://www.thetubestore.com/lib/thetubestore/schematics/Fender/Fender-Deluxe-5E3-Schematic.pdf)
- **Sources:** [rr-5e3](https://robrobinette.com/How_The_5E3_Deluxe_Works.htm)

### 8. A small transformer that compresses

- **Anchor:** Chassis, output transformer
- **Opening insight:** The output transformer is small for the job and saturates at high volume.
- **Why it matters to a player:** It is one reason a cranked Deluxe sounds squashed and thick.
- **Deeper notes:** 8k primary to 8-ohm secondary. Large 0.1 µF coupling capacitors pass a lot of low end, which adds to sag and can make the bass go soft.
- **Schematic check:** Coupling capacitors confirmed: .1 µF after the first stage and .1 µF to each 6V6 grid. The capacitor between the second stage and the inverter is unlabelled on the schematic; the layout shows a .02 µF 600 V part in that position. The 8k primary is not on the schematic. Not found. [fender-5e3-schem](https://www.thetubestore.com/lib/thetubestore/schematics/Fender/Fender-Deluxe-5E3-Schematic.pdf)
- **Sources:** [rr-5e3](https://robrobinette.com/How_The_5E3_Deluxe_Works.htm)

### 9. One Jensen and a hidden jack

- **Anchor:** Cabinet rear, speaker
- **Opening insight:** The speaker jack and an extension jack are tucked under the chassis.
- **Why it matters to a player:** An 8-ohm extension cabinet changes the load, which changes how the amp feels.
- **Deeper notes:** The speaker jack shorts the secondary when nothing is plugged in. With an 8-ohm extension speaker the total load becomes 4 ohms.
- **Schematic check:** Confirmed. The schematic draws a speaker jack with a shorting contact and a second jack marked "EXTRA 8 ohm SPKR". The layout places "SPKR. JACK" and "EXTRA SPKR." below the board. The 4-ohm total is arithmetic. [fender-5e3-schem](https://www.thetubestore.com/lib/thetubestore/schematics/Fender/Fender-Deluxe-5E3-Schematic.pdf)
- **Sources:** [rr-5e3](https://robrobinette.com/How_The_5E3_Deluxe_Works.htm) [mojo-5e3](https://mojotone.com/blogs/fender-field-guide/fender-narrow-panel-tweed-deluxe)

## Uncertainty

- Second pass: the Fender 5E3 schematic and layout (drawing F-EE) were read. Each hotspot now carries a "Schematic check" line. Values marked confirmed can be attributed to the Fender drawing.
- Corrected from first pass: hotspot 6 said the cathode bypass value was not given. The Fender drawings give 25 µF, 25 V.
- Not found on the Fender drawings: supply voltages and the output transformer primary impedance. Both remain analysis figures.
- The analysis notes that one widely used reproduction schematic swaps V1A and V1B. The Fender schematic read here does not label triode halves, so it does not settle channel-to-triode assignment.
- The copy read is a retailer's scan. Its drawing title and number match Fender practice, but the scan's own provenance is not documented.
- Interior positions on a real chassis are still not established. The layout gives relative order, not measured positions.
- A 1957 specimen with interior photos was not found in this pass. The licensed chassis photo is undated and has replaced capacitors.
- Do not repeat artist associations without a source in the repo.
