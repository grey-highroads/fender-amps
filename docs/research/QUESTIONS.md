# Open questions

Researched 2026-11-20. Second pass 2026-11-20. Ranked by what each one blocks: modeling first, then content, then neither. Each question now carries a status: resolved, partly resolved or still open.

## Blocks modeling

### Q1. Dual Professional has no cabinet dimensions.

**Status: resolved, with estimates.** A 1947 dealer specimen is listed at 23 x 20 x 9 in and Fender's 2023 recreation at 19.75 high x 22.38 wide x 9.88 deep, so the amp is no longer blocked; neither is a factory figure for the vintage amp.

### Q2. Photographs were not opened.

**Status: partly resolved.** Galleries were opened for every amp and the real views, caveats and direct image URLs are in each brief, but most were read as contact sheets and six amps still lack a chassis interior view of a matching specimen.

Front, rear, side, panel and interior of a matching dated specimen are now covered for the Dual Professional, Tremolux, Champ, Showman and Super Reverb. Still short: Deluxe 6G3, Super Six, The Twin, Vibro-King, Blues Junior and Pano Verb have no chassis interior; the 5E3 and Princeton Reverb have no dated specimen with interior photos.

### Q3. Showman: which cabinet, which speaker.

**Status: partly resolved.** Two 1961 6G14-A amps eight serial numbers apart are documented, one a 12 and one a 15, and the 15 is sold with what its dealer calls the original JBL D130; choosing between them is now a decision, and no Fender document for the speaker model was read.

November 21 builder update: the owner-authorized batch selects the single fifteen.
Fender's later 1962–63 catalog confirms the fifteen-inch Lansing format without a
model suffix. Exact 1961 suffix remains open; the model uses a generic cast-frame
fifteen, not the specimen's replacement D140F. Head depth is tracked in Q28.

### Q4. Deluxe 6G3: channel order and baffle fixing.

**Status: partly resolved.** A 1962 specimen reads Normal then Bright from left to right with no front baffle screws visible, and the Fender schematic settles the rectifier as GZ34; no early amp was found to confirm the other layout.

### Q5. Vibro-King height.

**Status: partly resolved.** The 25.5 in figure is Fender's own, in the manuals for both the original and the Custom, while a retailer says 22.5 and a 1993 specimen about 23; photos favour the lower figures for the cabinet body, so Fender's number may include handle and feet, but nothing states that.

### Q9. Tremolux 5E9 cabinet size.

**Status: resolved.** Three June 1955 amps from two dealers are in the Deluxe-size small box (one measured at 20 x 16 x 9.5 in), and later amps are in a larger box (one 1959 amp at 20 high x 22 wide x 10.5 deep), so a first-batch 1955 model uses the small box. Rechecked 2026-11-20: RetroFret gives 22 x 20 x 10.5 for serial 0093, but its own front photo measures 1.25 to 1, which fits the small box, and its text says the cabinet matched the Deluxe. See [small-amp checks](packets/small-amp-checks.md).

## Blocks content

### Q6. Princeton Reverb is not on the timeline.

**Status: resolved by the site owner on 2026-11-20.** The Princeton Reverb becomes a timeline addition; it still needs a runtime entry, a catalog mapping for CSV row 67 and an assembly before its explorer hotspots can be used.

### Q7. Princeton Reverb AA1164 rectifier.

**Status: open. Does not block the exterior.** The Fender AA1164 schematic, drawing K-FD, shows a 5U4GB. A very early 1965 amp's tube chart reads AA764 and draws a GZ34. The main modeling specimen's bottle is a later replacement and is not evidence of factory fitment. Keep the disagreement in the hotspot; the model bottle is a labelled placeholder.

### Q8. Champ 5F1 timeline year.

**Status: still open.** No new evidence changes it; a measured April 1959 specimen confirms the 8-inch cabinet at 12 x 13.5 x 7.5 in, and the year remains a labelling decision.

### Q10. The Twin: thin evidence.

**Status: partly resolved.** One page of the owner's manual and one original-owner 1988 specimen were read, which confirm the feature set and the 4, 8 or 16 ohm selector, but no Fender dimensions, no dated standard-covering specimen and no answer on the 1985 versus 1987 introduction were found.

### Q12. Primary schematics were not read.

**Status: partly resolved.** The Fender 5E3 schematic and layout, the AA1164 schematic and the AB763 Super Reverb schematic were read and every hotspot value in those three briefs is marked confirmed, corrected or not found; the Fender layouts for the AA1164 and the Super Reverb were not found.

Corrections that came out of it: the 5E3 cathode bypass is 25 µF 25 V (first pass said not given); the Princeton Reverb output transformer is 125A10B (first pass said no number found); the Super Reverb mid capacitors are .022 µF in both channels. Not on any Fender drawing read: 5E3 supply voltages and transformer impedances.

### Q14. Display-ready photographs.

**Status: partly resolved.** Seven Wikimedia Commons images with redistribution licences are recorded in the briefs, all representative, none of the exact year and circuit being modelled. Third check 2026-11-20 for the 5E3: no freely licensed photo of a genuine dated 5E3 was found; the Commons "Tweed deluxe" chassis is a handmade replica, and the only real narrow-panel Deluxe found (Flickr, blackjetts) is CC BY-NC 2.0. See the [5E3 packet](packets/deluxe-5e3-explorer.md).

## Blocks neither

### Q11. Super Six load figure.

**Status: still open.** Not researched further; a dealer page repeats 5.2 ohms and says Fender used the Twin's 4-ohm output transformer.

### Q13. Dual Professional introduction date.

**Status: still open.** Fender's manual for the 1990s amp of the same name says the first Dual Professional appeared in 1946, Mojotone says 1947 with the rename to Super in fall 1947, and the catalog says 1947 to 1948.

## New in the second pass

### Q15. The Twin: knob colour by year.

**Status: open. Blocks modeling detail.** Sources give red until 1994, or red or black from 1990, and an original owner says a 1988 limited-covering amp has original black knobs. A dated standard black amp or a Fender catalogue page would settle it.

### Q16. Dual Professional: power and preamp tube count.

**Status: open. Blocks content.** Output is 16, 18 or 26 W and the preamp is two or three 6SJ7 depending on the source. Mojotone's 26 W matches the 2023 recreation and may have been taken from it.

### Q17. Fender layouts for the AA1164 Princeton Reverb and AB763 Super Reverb.

**Status: open. Blocks interior placement.** Only the schematics were found. Hotspot anchors inside the chassis still rest on secondary drawings and, for the Super Reverb, one specimen's chassis photos.

### Q18. Tremolux 5E9 tube count.

**Status: resolved 2026-11-20.** Six tubes. The June 1955 tube chart in serial 0093 lists a printed 5U4GA corrected by hand to 5Y3GT, two 6V6GT, two 12AX7 and a 12AY7, and the Fender 5E9-A drawing (D-EE) shows the same six with a 5Y3GT. The hand correction is evidence for that amp; it does not prove every early 5E9 shipped with a 5Y3GT. No 5E9 drawing was found. See [small-amp checks](packets/small-amp-checks.md).

## New in the third pass

### Q19. Pro TV front: no Fender 5A5 drawing.

**Status: open. Blocks content.** The only Fender sheet found is an unnumbered "PRO-AMP" drawing filed as 5B5; its power transformer number matches a dealer-described 1949 5A5, which suggests a close circuit but does not prove it.

### Q20. Fender 75: which format, and how big.

**Status: open. Blocks modeling.** The catalog and field guide list a 1x12 combo, a 1x15 combo and a head; the only specimen viewed is a 1x12, and the published figures disagree on height (21 in for the 1x15 in the field guide, 23.2 in in a database of unstated format).

### Q21. Vibroverb 6G16: output and dating.

**Status: open. Blocks content.** Output is 35 W in the catalog and Wikipedia and about 45 W on a dealer listing, and the dealer's one measured specimen (serial 00433) is dated mid-1963 on one page and early 1964 on another.

### Q22. Black-panel Bassman cabinet height.

**Status: open. Blocks modeling detail.** The field guide gives 21 x 32 x 11½ in and a reader correction says 20 x 32 x 11; no specimen was measured.

### Q23. No shared assemblies for the Bassman 5F6-A and Vibroverb 6G16.

**Status: partly resolved November 21.** The owner authorized and the builder implemented a shared 5F6-A assembly and nine topics; its absence blocker is resolved upon publication. The Vibroverb 6G16 still needs its own assembly. The following is historical context: both were on the timeline. Main now has six assemblies (5E3, Deluxe Reverb, 1968 Twin, Champ, early Tremolux and brown Deluxe), none of which is a 4x10 or a 2x10 brown cabinet, so hotspot anchors need a new assembly first.

### Q24. Bassman 5B6 dimensions.

**Status: open. Blocks modeling.** No published or measured figure was found; the brief uses the 1949 TV-front Pro as a stand-in on the strength of one magazine saying the cabinets were similar in size.

## New in the fourth pass

### Q25. Champ 5F1 depth: 8 in or 7.5 in.

**Status: open. Does not block.** RetroFret measures C-12147 at 8 in deep and C-10063 at 7.5 in, in the same format. An underside photo of C-10063 supports about 7.7 in; C-12147 has no photo that shows depth. No measuring convention explains the gap. See [small-amp checks](packets/small-amp-checks.md).

### Q26. Princeton Reverb: which grille, logo or no logo.

**Status: resolved as a modeling choice 2026-11-20.** The main specimen (A-03894, output transformer week 7 of 1965) has the raised logo; a very early 1965 amp does not. Model the logo; the no-logo look is an early-1965 variant. See the [Princeton packet](packets/princeton-reverb-modeling.md).

### Q27. Bassman 5B6: is the 1952 TV-front amp a 5B6 at all?

**Status: open. Needs an owner or builder decision.** Fender's own history says the 1952 TV-front amp appeared in sales literature labelled "Amplifier", and that the 5B6 was the 1953 wide-panel restyle of it, essentially the same amp. The brief and catalog row 13 pair "5B6" with the 1952 TV front, and the Fender drawing read for the brief is headed only "BASSMAN" with no number. Either model the 1952 TV front without the 5B6 name, or switch to the 1953 wide panel and keep it. Fender also says the 1952 amp was its first closed-back amp, with two ports, a Jensen P15N and a bottom-mounted, copper-plated chassis; dimensions and output are not given. [fender-bassman-history](https://www.fender.com/articles/parts-and-accessories/going-low-the-history-of-the-bassman)

### Q28. Selected-year Vibrasonic width and Showman head depth.

**Status: open; blocks exact dimensional acceptance, not educational construction.**
November 21 builder read the 1962–63 Fender catalog, PDF page 4, at full page size.
It gives Vibrasonic width 26 inches against the early field-guide 25, and Showman
head depth 11½ inches against the field-guide 9. The models select 1960 5G13 and
1961 6G14-A respectively; the later catalog is not automatically applied to those
years. Need a selected-year factory document or actual cabinet measurement.
Both differences are disclosed in profiles, catalog mapping and fine print.

### Q29. Bassman 5F6-A inverter label in I-EG drawing pair.

**Status: open; blocks an unqualified tube-label assertion, not the assembly.**
November 21 visual read: the layout labels the inverter 12AX7 while the schematic
label appears 12AY7. The topology and surrounding component values are clear.
Model follows the layout and discloses the discrepancy in profile/card fine print.
Need an authoritative revision clarification or sharper independent Fender copy;
do not silently erase either label. Existing brief's statement that all tube
labels agree is superseded by this current check.
